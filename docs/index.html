Deep Space Strategy

We want to understand how to establish the frontier in space and make space work.

Science fiction has sparked many visions of how humanity could settle space, from mining colonies in the asteroid belt to cities on Mars. Reality has moved more slowly. Nobody has walked on the Moon since December 1972, almost 54 years ago. Every mission beyond Earth orbit still runs on government money.

Open questions

  • Do people need to settle space themselves, now that autonomous machines could take on the heavy work?
  • Which economic forces shape the frontier, and what would make private investment in it pay?
  • Where should the first infrastructure go, and in which order?
  • Which rules for shared infrastructure let the frontier grow, and how can competition stay peaceful?

How we work

Map

solmaps, our open engine, computes what it costs to move mass between any two places in the solar system on any day until 2100, and maps where water and metals are.

Strategy

From the map and the economics we derive where to build first and in which order. We also study which rules suit shared infrastructure and how competition can stay peaceful, and test these strategies in simulations with many actors. The results form a primer for settling the solar system, based on evidence.

Method

The maps and route tools run on solmaps, our open-source engine. How solmaps works gives an overview; the technical documentation gives the formulas and references.

The route calculations use the standard methods of early mission planning. They match the launch and flyby dates of the Cassini mission to within a month, and those of Juno, Galileo and Dawn to within three months.
